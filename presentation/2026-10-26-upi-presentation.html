  1. Build a clean, analysis-ready monthly dataset from NPCI's published statistics.
  2. Measure growth, payment size and seasonality with simple metrics that can be checked by hand.
  3. Test the slowdown formally with a regression that treats time-series errors correctly.

Year-on-year growth over time, and a regression with a curved trend.

One regression: is growth really slowing?

  

  

    

       = β₀ + β₁· + β₂· +  + δ· + 
    

    
t² · the key term
If β₂ is negative, the growth rate itself falls over time. Implied annual growth at month t = e^(12·(β₁ + 2β₂t)) − 1.

    

      
Why start in April 2019?
2016 to 2018 was the launch phase: banks onboarding, demonetisation, the BHIM app. Growth ran at 300% to 1000%+. A smooth curve cannot fit that and the mature phase together. The result holds for start dates from 2017 to 2020.

      
Why Newey-West (HAC) errors?
Monthly errors are autocorrelated (Durbin-Watson 0.70; 2 means none). Ordinary standard errors would be too small. cov_type='HAC' corrects them: the coefficients are unchanged, the standard errors become 1.8× larger.

    

  

  
statsmodels · smf.ols(...).fit(cov_type='HAC', cov_kwds={'maxlags': 12}) · n = 90 months

Key findings

    
Five findings

  

  

    
127→16%
Growth is decelerating, and the regression confirms it
t² is negative and significant in every sample. A classic S-curve of adoption.

    
−31%
UPI is growing in count, not in money
Volume ×30.8, value ×20.7 since 2019. The average payment fell from ₹1,870 to ₹1,282.

    
+4.2%
Raw monthly totals mislead
Per day, February sits above trend. October to December are the real peak.

    
−26.7%
COVID was a short, sharp shock
The 2020 lockdown cut volume below trend for two months, then growth resumed.

    
0.98 / 0.42
"More banks, more UPI" is mostly a shared trend
Correlation in levels vs in growth rates. Bank onboarding explains only part of adoption.

Limitations, future scope and conclusion

    
A maturing system of everyday payments

  

  

    

      
Limitations

      
    
        
  • National totals only: no split by merchant vs person-to-person, state or app.
    • 
        
  • Ticket size is a mean; a few large payments can move it.
    • 
        
  • The t² trend describes the slowdown in-sample. It is not a forecast.
    • 
        
  • COVID dummy covers 2020 only; the 2021 wave shows in the residuals.
    • 
      

    

    

      
Future scope

      
    
        
  • Add NPCI's merchant (P2M) vs person (P2P) split.
    • 
        
  • Fit a logistic S-curve to estimate the saturation level.
    • 
        
  • Compare with RBI data on cash in circulation.
    • 
      

    

    

      Conclusion
      
UPI has moved from early adopters to everyday small payments. Growth is slowing, payments are shrinking, and the system is maturing.
